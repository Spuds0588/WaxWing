Why WaxWing

The studio quality of StreamYard & Riverside — without the SaaS bill

Zero backend

Everything runs in the browser from a static site. No database, no SFU, no subscription — nothing to provision or pay for.

Truly peer to peer

Guests connect straight to your browser over WebRTC. Once the link is open, audio and video never touch an intermediary.

Guests tap a magic link

No accounts, no downloads. Anyone with a browser — including phones — joins from the link your studio hands you.

4K recorded to your disk

Your camera’s full-res feed streams straight into a file on your machine via the File System Access API. No upload bottleneck.

Director’s Cut

Drag, drop, and resize feeds on a 16:9 stage — cameras, and anyone’s shared screen — then capture exactly what you arranged in a 1080p region recording of the show.

Files come to you

When the show stops, each guest’s high-res master is streamed peer-to-peer into your folder. Everything lands in one place.

Make it yours

Upload a background and logo, recolor the stage accents, hide or show name tags and chips, and save custom tile arrangements as templates — baked into the broadcast and your Director’s Cut.

FAQ

Quick answers

Is WaxWing really free? Do I need an account?

Yes — free, with no sign-up. The host opens the app in a browser and guests join through a shared magic link. There’s no backend server or database anywhere in the stack.

Where are recordings saved?

On each participant’s own device. On Chrome/Edge desktop the raw camera feed is written straight to a folder you choose; after the show, guests’ high-res masters are transferred to the host peer-to-peer.

What browsers are supported?

Full functionality — 4K direct-to-disk recording and the 1080p Director’s Cut — needs Chromium on desktop (Chrome, Edge, Brave). Guests can join from Safari, Firefox, and mobile browsers with graceful degradation.

How is this different from Zoom or Riverside?

No cloud recording, no uploads, no subscription. WaxWing is a peer-to-peer studio: the composited stage you direct is the broadcast, and every full-quality master is recorded locally by the person who owns the camera.
